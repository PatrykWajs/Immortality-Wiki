# The Longevity Effects Of Psilocybin

## Overview

Bryan Johnson explores the potential longevity benefits of psilocybin (the psychoactive compound in magic mushrooms), conducting the most quantified psychedelic experiment ever recorded. By rigorously tracking 249+ biomarkers, Johnson examines how psilocybin could influence biological aging, stress, brain function, metabolism, inflammation, and the gut microbiome in an already highly optimized body.

## Key Insights

- Johnson took two measured doses of psilocybin: first, 4.67g dried B+ mushrooms (24.98mg psilocybin); second, 5.35g (28mg) three weeks later.
- 249 biomarkers tracked, including brain activity, hormones, inflammation, metabolism, heart health, sexual function, DNA, aging, and microbiome.
- Psilocybin induced a sharp cortisol (stress hormone) spike during the experience, but both morning cortisol and DHEA levels dropped 42% and 45% respectively for days after, indicating a sustained 'afterglow' of calm.
- Continuous glucose monitoring showed mean blood sugar dropped by 8%, variability by 11%, and estimated HbA1c moved from 4.7 to 4.4 — shifting from the top 2% to the top 0.2% of 18–25-year-olds after a single dose.
- Inflammation marker (hs-CRP) dropped from 0.23 to below detectable levels post-dose, and stayed suppressed after the second, indicating erased chronic inflammation.
- Brain imaging via Kernel Flow device revealed reduced activity in the default mode network, increased neural entropy, and greater flexibility and integration—suggesting a more 'youthful' brain profile.
- The experiment delivered the first documented thermal imaging of a psilocybin experience and the first continuous glucose evidence of metabolic improvement in humans.
- Gut microbiome composition shifted: species linked to stress and insulin resistance decreased, while new beneficial species appeared.
- These sustained effects—across stress response, inflammation, metabolism, brain function, and microbiome—were observed in a subject already optimized for health.
- Protocol was medically supervised, legal, and highly controlled; not intended as a recommendation or endorsement for unsupervised use.

## Core Concepts

- **Quantified Psilocybin Protocol:** Extensive pre- and post-dose measurement of 249+ biomarkers to assess real physiological impact, not just subjective experience.
- **Neural Entropy:** Measure of brain adaptability and connectivity; increased entropy reflects less rigid and more youthful, flexible brain network patterns after psilocybin.
- **Afterglow Effect:** Documented phase of biochemical calm and lowered stress reactivity (cortisol, DHEA) persisting for days following a psychedelic experience.
- **Metabolic Reset:** Significant improvements in blood sugar regulation (glucose, HbA1c) occurring rapidly after psilocybin, typically only seen after months of pharmaceutical intervention.
- **Systems-Level Reset:** Coordinated improvements across key biological drivers of aging—stress response, brain function, metabolic health, inflammation, and gut microbiome.

## Practical Takeaways

- Carefully monitored and medically supervised psychedelic interventions may produce multi-system benefits relevant to longevity, notably in stress, inflammation, and metabolism.
- Measuring objective biomarkers before, during, and after any novel therapy is crucial to separate subjective experience from actual physiological effects.
- Openness to testing interventions outside conventional paradigms (while remaining safety-focused) can reveal previously overlooked pathways to health optimization.
- Integration of technologies like neuroimaging and continuous glucose monitoring enables more precise understanding of interventions' impacts.
- Core longevity practices (sleep, nutrition, exercise, discipline) remain foundational—novel interventions should be seen as complementary, not replacements.
- Psychological and identity shifts are real risks; deliberate preparation and support are essential for any significant psychedelic or experimental protocol.
- Data transparency and rigorous documentation can help destigmatize promising therapies and foster scientific progress.

## Key Learnings & Conclusions

Bryan Johnson’s experiment provides first-in-human evidence that psilocybin, when taken in a rigorously controlled and measured setting, exerts measurable improvements across multiple biological systems tied to aging—even in an already optimized individual. These changes—reduced inflammation, improved blood sugar control, lower stress hormone levels, greater brain network flexibility, and positive microbiome shifts—suggest psilocybin could be a legitimate longevity intervention, deserving further robust study. While not a panacea, psilocybin’s ability to measurably impact aging determinants opens new avenues for evidence-based health optimization.
